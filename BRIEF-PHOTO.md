# Showcase brief, round 4 — 5 restaurant sites built on REAL PHOTOGRAPHS (360° photo scenes)

The user looked at the realistic round (`BRIEF-3D-REAL.md`) and said it still looks animated; they want a real-world look. The chosen approach: **real 360° photographs are the scenery**, real scanned props sit in front of them, the photo's own light lights those props, and the camera glides, rotates and cross-fades between photographed places like a virtual tour. Read first: `BRIEF.md`, `BRIEF-3D.md`, `BRIEF-3D-REAL.md` (all rules still apply: lazy import, WebGL probe and poster fallback, quality tiers, pause button, reduced motion, skip link focus, contrast over the scene, no invented facts, footer line, loop guards, context restore, no debug hooks, phone menu that scrolls, short-screen unpin) and skim `qa/CODE-REVIEW-REAL.md` and `qa/CODE-REVIEW-3D-R2.md` for bugs to avoid.

## Fastest start (use it)
Each site already exists in a finished, reviewed, accessibility-fixed form: `sites/himal-chulo`, `sites/ember-and-oak`, `sites/flour-and-fig`, `sites/olive-and-sea`, `sites/jade-teahouse`. Create your NEW folder `sites/<slug>-photo/` by COPYING the matching old folder, then keep its `index.html`, `style.css`, copy, menu, pause button, focus handling, posters' structure and `assets/CREDITS.txt` pattern, and REPLACE the 3D world (`scene.js` / `world.js`) with a photo-panorama scene as described below. Do not edit the old folder. Change the page title, the `Sample design` wording stays. Update `assets/CREDITS.txt` for the panoramas and props you really use.

## The recipe
1. **Panoramas (the scenery).** Get real photos with the helper: `python showcase/tools/fetch_polyhaven.py pano <id>` writes `showcase/assets/polyhaven/pano/<id>/<id>_8k.jpg` (desktop, about 3-6 MB), `<id>_4k.jpg` (phones, about 1.5 MB) and `<id>_1k.hdr` (lighting). Browse ids at `https://api.polyhaven.com/assets?t=hdris` (filter by `&c=indoor`, `outdoor`, `night`, tags). LOOK at each candidate (open the `_4k.jpg` with the Read tool) and choose by eye: pick scenes where the part of the photo you will frame shows tables, floor and atmosphere, not clutter. Use 2 to 4 panoramas per site, one per mood or location, not one per station.
   Candidates (verify by looking; pick the best, or others you find): himal-chulo-photo: `warm_restaurant_night`, `warm_restaurant`, `courtyard_night`, mountain dusk views `qwantani_dusk_1`, `fouriesburg_mountain_lookout`. ember-and-oak-photo: `fireplace` (has a real fire), `warm_bar`, `wooden_lounge`, `cabin`. flour-and-fig-photo: `comfy_cafe`, `medieval_cafe`, `newman_cafeteria`. olive-and-sea-photo: `lythwood_terrace`, `outdoor_umbrellas`, `hotel_rooftop_balcony`, `sundowner_deck`, `courtyard`. jade-teahouse-photo: `chinese_garden`, `warm_reception_dinner`, `wooden_lounge`.
2. **Show the photo as the world.** An inward-facing sphere with the equirectangular texture (sRGB, `ClampToEdge`, mipmaps, anisotropy up to 8), or `vendor/three-addons/objects/GroundProjectedSkybox.js` (importmap `three/addons/objects/GroundProjectedSkybox.js`) so the floor in the photo becomes a real ground plane at a set eye height and props can stand on it in correct perspective. Camera at the sphere centre (or at a modest height above the projected ground); move by rotating (yaw and pitch), changing FOV (a "dolly zoom" feel: 30-55°) and, with ground projection, small forward travel. Keep moves slow and deliberate. Cross-dissolve between panoramas (two spheres with a mix uniform or opacity) at the story's transitions; keep at most TWO panoramas in GPU memory at once and dispose the old one after the dissolve (an 8k texture is about 170 MB on the GPU with mipmaps). Phones: 4k panoramas only (many phone GPUs cap textures at 4096).
3. **Real props in front.** Real Poly Haven scans (`python showcase/tools/fetch_polyhaven.py model <id> 1k`) placed on a real wooden table model or on the projected floor, large enough in frame to matter (macro and close-up stations are where props live; wide stations are mostly the photo). Light them with the photo itself: PMREM from the panorama's `_1k.hdr` as `scene.environment` (or the baked-PMREM trick used in `sites/jade-teahouse/assets/` to avoid the 5-7 s PMREM freeze: bake once offline, ship the small result), one key light matching the photo's brightest light direction with soft shadows, a `ShadowMaterial` shadow-catcher under every prop, matching exposure and white balance. The props must look like they were photographed in the room: match colour temperature, add a touch of film grain and a gentle vignette over the whole frame, slight depth-of-field or blur on the far background if it holds frame rate.
4. **Honesty and licence.** The panoramas are photographs of real places by Poly Haven photographers (CC0). Do not name or describe the real venue and do not imply it is the client's restaurant; the site is a sample. Credit every panorama and prop in `assets/CREDITS.txt` (the helper logs to `showcase/assets/polyhaven/CREDITS.md`). Food: Poly Haven has no cooked dishes. Keep any modelled dish small and well lit or partly hidden (steam, depth of field, a lid, a shadowed corner); let real props and the photograph carry the realism; keep the 'Illustrative sample menu' label. Animation that is not photographic (flames, steam, bulbs switching on) should be subtle, grounded and lit like the photo, never cartoony. Prayer flags, string lights, bunting and similar small CG add-ons must have physically plausible cloth or glass shading.
5. **Honest limit.** A panorama is a still photograph: the camera cannot walk freely through it. Design the scroll journey around rotations, FOV pushes and cross-dissolves between photographed viewpoints, plus real props in the foreground. Say in your report what still looks CG.

## Budgets
Desktop: the first station under about 6 MB before the first frame, the whole site at most about 20 MB streamed in idle time. Phone: at most about 8 MB total. First 3D frame within about 3 s on a quiet GPU; no main-thread task over about 400 ms (build in chunks, `compileAsync` against the real render target, `checkShaderErrors = false`, decode images off the main thread with `createImageBitmap`, upload textures one per task, texture upload of the panorama itself is a long task: do it in the loading state with the poster shown). Frame rate should be far better than the previous round because the world is one textured sphere: target 60+ fps on the dev GPU; report medians of 3.

## Verify before reporting (headless only: the user's screen must never show a browser window)
Same checks as `BRIEF-3D-REAL.md`: 0 console and page errors at 1440, 1024, 390, 720x450, 390x667; no horizontal scroll; WebGL off (posters, stable page height); reduced motion (still frames, 0 idle rAF); pause (0 rAF while paused, identical frames, resume restores everything); context loss and restore (panorama and environment come back); skip link and menu focus; phone menu scrolls at 320x256; panels do not cover the scene on phones; contrast measured against the real rendered pixels at every station (panoramas have bright windows and lamps: panels must be solid enough); bytes requested; startup timings. SCREENSHOT every station at 1440 and 390x844 and LOOK at them: the test is whether a stranger would take it for a real place. Iterate on grade, light matching, shadows and prop placement until it does. Report honestly what still looks CG.
