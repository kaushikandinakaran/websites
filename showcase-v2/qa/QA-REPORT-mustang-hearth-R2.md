## Placeholder judgement (asked by the manager)
Yes, the two hero tags make the first screen look unfinished. A client judges the design in its first 5 seconds, and monospace bracket notes beside the H1 read as "not done". My recommendation is to split placeholders by type:
- **Facts stay visibly marked**, because inventing them would be a lie: `[address]`, `[phone]`, `[email]`, `[map]`, "(sample hours)", `[Guest reviews: client to supply]`, `[Press or awards…]`, `[Your story…]` (who cooks here is a fact), delivery-app names. These are already styled as dashed slots in the story section (story-placeholders-1440.jpg), which looks deliberate and is fine.
- **Wording stays clean.** The promise line and "Order direct with us and skip the app fees" are sample marketing copy, not facts, so remove the inline `[…: client to edit]` tags. Keep the honesty in two places: (1) extend the footer line on every page to "Sample design by Website Studio. Menu, prices and wording are samples. Photos are stock." and (2) list the editable lines in the site's hand-off notes (or as `data-client-edit` attributes the studio tool can list). The brief's rule 6 ("skip the app fees ONLY as wording the client can edit") is then still met: no percentage or saving is claimed, and the copy is flagged as editable sample copy.
- If the manager wants an on-page mark anyway, use one small superscript dagger (†) with a footer key "† sample wording, client to edit", not a monospace tag.
